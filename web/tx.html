All three occupy the same bands and share the same coding, framing and MODCOD ladder; only the physical layer differs. QAM is the default and the best on a linear path. OFDM is the one for delay spread. APSK trades about 0.8 dB of minimum distance for a lower peak-to-average ratio — see the README for what that is measured to be worth, which on a pulse-shaped single carrier is less than the satellite literature would lead you to expect.
Symbol rate, MODCOD and bitrate are three views of one number: fix any two and the third follows. Lock what you want held, edit anything, and whatever is still free absorbs the change. Locking the symbol rate is the HD Radio arrangement — a fixed slice of spectrum, with the MODCOD moving to fill it.
The one dial OFDM has. The band is already fixed, so the carrier count is the subcarrier spacing: fewer carriers means wider spacing, which a drifting transmitter cannot push a signal out of, and a shorter guard interval, which a room echo walks straight past. More carriers trades it the other way. Throughput barely moves either way — that is set by the bandwidth, not by how finely it is sliced. Must match at the receiver.
